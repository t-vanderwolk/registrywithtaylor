# ChatGPT Prompts — Free Baby Prep Starter Guide + Welcome Email

Two ready-to-paste prompts. Fill in anything in `[BRACKETS]` first (links, dates), then paste into ChatGPT. Run **Prompt 1** to make the guide, then **Prompt 2** to make the welcome email that delivers it.

Reusable brand context is baked into both so the output already sounds like Taylor-Made Baby Co.

---

## Prompt 1 — Free Baby Prep Starter Guide (quick-start checklist)

```
You are an expert baby registry consultant and warm, plain-spoken copywriter writing for Taylor-Made Baby Co. (taylormadebabyco.com), founded by Taylor Vanderwolk — a Baby Registry Consultant, Certified Baby Gear Specialist, and verified Target Baby Concierge specialist.

BRAND VOICE
- Warm, calm, and reassuring — like a knowledgeable friend, not a salesperson.
- Practical and decisive: help parents know "what to buy, what to skip, and what can wait."
- No fear-mongering, no overwhelm, no jargon. Confidence over pressure.
- Editorial but easy to skim.

AUDIENCE
First-time expecting parents (roughly 2nd–3rd trimester) who feel overwhelmed by registries and baby gear and want a trustworthy shortcut.

TASK
Write the "Free Baby Prep Starter Guide" — a QUICK-START CHECKLIST lead magnet, about 2–3 pages when formatted. It should feel like a generous, genuinely useful free gift that makes the reader think "I need to work with her."

STRUCTURE (use these sections, with short intros + checkbox lists)
1. Title page line: a warm, benefit-driven title and one-sentence subtitle.
2. "A quick note from Taylor" — 3–4 warm sentences: you get how overwhelming this is, here's how to use this guide, you've got this.
3. "Start here: 5 things to do first" — the highest-leverage early steps (e.g., pick ONE registry, set a rough budget, note your lifestyle, etc.).
4. "The real essentials, by category" — short checklists for: Sleep, Feeding, Diapering, Travel (car seat + stroller), Nursery, and Postpartum/Home prep. Under each, list the true must-haves only.
5. "What you can skip or wait on" — a short, honest list of commonly over-bought items and why.
6. "The one-page starter checklist" — a clean, printable checkbox list summarizing the essentials.
7. "A simple budgeting tip" — one calm paragraph on prioritizing spend.
8. "When you want a shortcut" — a soft CTA: book a $75 Virtual Registry Consultation (1 hour, personalized), and try the free baby gear tools (Stroller Finder, Car Seat + Stroller Compatibility Checker, Stroller Quiz). Use the placeholders [BOOKING LINK] and [TOOLS LINK].

CONSTRAINTS
- Keep it skimmable: short paragraphs, real checkbox lists (use "- [ ]"), clear headers.
- US context. Be safety-accurate on car seats (never suggest secondhand car seats or bypassing safety), but keep it light, not scary.
- Stay brand-agnostic — recommend CATEGORIES and what to look for, not specific brands (Taylor personalizes brands in the paid consult).
- Encouraging, never preachy. ~700–1,100 words total.

OUTPUT
Return clean Markdown ready to drop into a doc or Canva. Use "#" for the title, "##" for sections. Do not include this prompt in the output.
```

## Prompt 2 — Welcome + Guide Delivery Email

```
You are an email copywriter for Taylor-Made Baby Co. (taylormadebabyco.com), founded by Taylor Vanderwolk — a Baby Registry Consultant, Certified Baby Gear Specialist, and verified Target Baby Concierge specialist.

BRAND VOICE
- Warm, personal, and calm — like a friend who happens to be the expert.
- Confident and reassuring; "what to buy, what to skip, what can wait."
- No hype, no spammy urgency, no jargon.

CONTEXT
This is the FIRST email a new subscriber receives, immediately after they sign up to download the "Free Baby Prep Starter Guide." Its jobs, in order: (1) deliver the guide, (2) make a warm first impression and establish trust, (3) set expectations for the newsletter, (4) gently point to the $75 consult and free tools.

TASK
Write one welcome email.

INCLUDE
- 3–5 subject line options (warm, benefit-forward, not clickbait) + 1 preview/preheader line.
- A warm greeting to a new subscriber.
- Deliver the guide right up top with a clear button/link: use placeholder [GUIDE DOWNLOAD LINK].
- 2–3 sentences introducing Taylor and why parents trust her (registry consultant + certified baby gear specialist + Target Baby Concierge) — credible but humble.
- "What to expect" — 1–2 sentences on the kind of helpful, no-overwhelm emails they'll get.
- ONE soft secondary CTA: mention the $75 Virtual Registry Consultation and the free baby gear tools, with placeholders [BOOKING LINK] and [TOOLS LINK]. Keep the guide as the primary action.
- A warm sign-off from Taylor.
- A short P.S. inviting them to simply reply with their due date or their biggest worry.

CONSTRAINTS
- Mobile-friendly and concise: ~150–250 words in the body (excluding subject lines).
- One primary CTA (the guide); everything else secondary.
- Keep any variable details as [BRACKETED] placeholders.
- Friendly, human, and skimmable — short paragraphs.

OUTPUT
Return the subject line options first (clearly labeled), then the preview line, then the full email body in plain text with light Markdown for the links/buttons. Do not include this prompt in the output.
```

### How to use
1. Replace the bracketed placeholders (`[GUIDE DOWNLOAD LINK]`, `[BOOKING LINK]`, `[TOOLS LINK]`) — or leave them for ChatGPT to keep as placeholders and swap in later.
2. Paste **Prompt 1** into ChatGPT → get the guide → refine with follow-ups like "make the intro warmer" or "tighten section 4."
3. Paste **Prompt 2** → get the welcome email → pick your favorite subject line.
4. Optional: after the guide exists, you can tell ChatGPT "here's the final guide: [paste]" before running Prompt 2 so the email references it accurately.
